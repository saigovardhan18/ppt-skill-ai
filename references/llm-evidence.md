# LLM content and evidence pipeline
1. Parse user materials, list source names and dates.
2. Extract atomic claims, figures, quotations, and their provenance.
3. Distinguish sourced facts, reasonable inference, and creative framing.
4. For numeric charts, verify every value and unit against the source; never synthesize numbers to make charts attractive.
5. Outline a narrative: context -> tension/question -> explanation/evidence -> implications -> takeaway.
6. Summarize for slide display; move nuance to speaker notes.
7. Include a source slide or notes when factual claims need attribution.
8. Mark unresolved claims for verification; avoid fake URLs, invented research, or unsupported certainty.
9. If external research is unavailable, work from supplied materials and say so.
10. Do not put sensitive uploaded data into external services without user authorization.

## Cross-reference: color system
Follow `color-psychology.md` (Johannes Itten, *The Art of Color*): choose a named contrast strategy, keep semantic color roles stable, and verify contrast. Do not infer unsupported psychological effects from hue.
